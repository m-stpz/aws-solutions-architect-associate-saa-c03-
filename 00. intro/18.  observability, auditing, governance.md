# Observability, Auditing, and Governance

- Distinct layers of operational control
  - Observability: system health and internal state
  - Auditing: accountability and forensic history
  - Governance: rules, guardrails, and compliance

## Observability - How is the system behaving? [System health & Internal State]

- Understand how a system is behaving internally and identify runtime failures
- "Why is the system slow or failing?"
- Software engineers, SREs, DevOps, on-call responders
- Real-time and immediate debugging
- CloudWatch, X-Ray, AWS Distro for OpenTelemetry

### Telemetry

- Metrics: aggregated numerical measurements over time
  - request throughput, p99 latency, error rates, queue depth
- Logs: timestamped, discrete records of specific events or errors emitted by applications and infra
- Traces: end-to-end records of a single request's journey across multiple services

## Auditing - Who did what when? [Accountability & Forensic History]

- Prove who did what, when, and from where with an immutable record
- "Who changed this resource or accessed this data?"
- Security teams, forensic investigators, external auditors, legal
- Historical and forenseic [post-event verification]
- AWS CloudTrail, VPC Flow Logs, S3 server access logs

## Governance - What is allowed? [Rules, Guardrails, and Compliance]

- Define and enforce what's allowed so the organization stays compliant and safe
- "Are all our systems adhering to our security, cost, and architectural rules?"
- Cloud architects, platform teams, CISO, compliance officers, FinOps
- Proactive [preventative guardrails] & continuous [drift detection]
- AWS organizations, AWS config, Control Tower, IAM
